Fact Pattern — Q3 · Herb, Wendy & Ann (California) · February 2004
In 1989, Herb and Wendy married while domiciled in Montana, a non-community property state. Prior to the marriage, Wendy had borrowed $25,000 from a Montana bank and executed a promissory note. Herb and Wendy, using savings from their salaries during their marriage, bought a Montana residence and took title as tenants in common.

In 1998, Herb and Wendy moved to California and became domiciled here. They did not sell their Montana house.

In 1999, Herb began having an affair with Ann. Herb told Ann he intended to divorce Wendy and marry her. Herb told Wendy he was moving to his own apartment because he "needed some space." Ann assumed Herb's last name; Herb introduced her to friends as his wife. Herb and Ann bought an automobile with a loan, listed as husband and wife on the application, and took title as husband and wife. Herb paid off the automobile loan out of his earnings.

Herb continued to spend occasional weekends with Wendy, who was unaware of his relationship with Ann. Wendy urged Herb to see a marriage counselor with her, which he did, without disclosing Ann.

In 2003, Wendy filed a petition for dissolution. At filing, the Montana bank was demanding $8,000 as the past-due balance on Wendy's note, reduced to a judgment. Also at filing, Ann had a $15,000 bank account in her name alone, solely from her earnings while living with Herb.
