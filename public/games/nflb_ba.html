BA · Agency Essay · Bell, Tome & Lamp (NCBE Practice)

Level 1
Fact Pattern — Is Principal Liable to Bell Seller, Tome Seller, and Lamp Seller?
Principal is an antiques dealer. As is common in the antiques business, Principal acquires inventory by using a group of buyers to purchase antiques on his behalf. Principal pays the buyers a percentage commission on the items they buy. Principal trains the buyers to evaluate potential purchases and sends them into the field with credentials identifying them as Principal's buyers, which they use to introduce themselves to sellers. The buyers enter into contracts using Principal's contract forms.

The Bell. Using Principal's credentials, Agent bought an antique church bell for $3,500 from Bell Seller, who believed Agent was acting on Principal's behalf. The church bell was on Principal's list and the price was within the range authorized by Principal. However, Agent did not intend to purchase the bell for Principal — he intended it for Greta, a competing antiques dealer, who agreed to pay Agent $250 to find such a bell. Agent intended to use Greta's money to pay for the bell. Greta rejected the bell. Principal refuses to pay Bell Seller.

The Tome. Agent had a written authorization from Principal to buy several books, including Looking Backward by Edward Bellamy. Agent showed this authorization to Tome Seller, who had a first edition. However, Agent did not tell Tome Seller that Principal had imposed a price limit on the purchase. Agent bought the book on Principal's behalf for $100 — above the limit. Principal refuses to pay Tome Seller.

The Lamp. When Principal learned of Agent's transactions, Principal sent Agent a letter terminating the agency. Several days after Agent received the letter, Agent purchased — for his own account — a whale oil lamp for $5,000 from Lamp Seller. Agent told Lamp Seller the purchase was for Principal's account, showed Lamp Seller his credentials from Principal, and used Principal's contract forms. Agent did not disclose that the agency had been terminated. Principal refuses to pay Lamp Seller, arguing Agent was no longer his buyer at the time of purchase.
