Fact Pattern — Question #1 · Harry & Wilma (California)
Shortly after graduating from college in 1957, Harry purchased a home for $40,000, taking title in his name. Harry's father gave him the $10,000 down payment and Harry borrowed the balance on a note secured by a 30-year mortgage. A month later, Harry married Wilma and began work for ABDO Corporation. Harry made all mortgage payments from his ABDO earnings. The house is now worth $500,000.

A homemaker until their children were grown, Wilma enrolled in a local university and in 1983 earned a professional credential in landscape architecture. Harry paid her tuition and other educational costs from his ABDO earnings.

In 1984, using a $50,000 inheritance from her father, Wilma established Flora, Inc. In 1990, Wilma was offered $600,000 for Flora even though the physical assets were worth only $50,000. Wilma rejected the offer.

In 1990, Harry was permanently and totally disabled in a hunting accident. In satisfaction of his personal injury claim, he received a $400,000 settlement, which he used to purchase a portfolio of corporate stocks now worth $600,000.

Harry now receives ABDO disability benefits providing two-thirds of his monthly pre-injury salary. Disability payments will continue until 1995 when Harry turns 60. He will then receive ABDO early retirement benefits equal to three-fourths of his pre-injury monthly salary.

Harry and Wilma have decided to obtain dissolution of their marriage.
