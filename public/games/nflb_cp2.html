Fact Pattern — Cal Bar July 2007
Husband and Wife married in 1997 in California. Neither brought any significant assets to the marriage, and they were both employed. H and W agreed that H should go to law school after they had saved up some money.

H put his earnings in a savings account in his name alone. W deposited her earnings into a joint checking account in both their names, which was used for their living expenses. H had a child support obligation from a previous marriage. Every month, H paid his child support by check from the joint checking account.

H began law school in 1998. W continued to support the couple. H took out a student loan to pay his tuition. H graduated in 2001 and obtained his law degree. He passed the bar and got a position with a large law firm.

In 2004, H became a partner in the firm. H's partnership earnings were substantial. He paid off his student loan with these earnings. Although the actual value of H's share was substantially greater, the partnership agreement provided that its value was $3,000 for purposes of valuation as marital property in the event of dissolution of a partner's marriage.

In 2006, H and W filed for dissolution of marriage.

Check every legal issue raised across all four questions: (a) child support reimbursement, (b) student loan reimbursement, (c) community interest in law degree, (d) goodwill and partnership valuation.
