Fact Pattern — Q4 · Herb & Wendy / CoinCo (California) · February 2010
Herb and Wendy, residents of California, married in 2001. Herb worked as an accountant. Wendy was an avid coin collector who hoped to turn her hobby into a business. Prior to marriage, they entered into a prenuptial agreement providing that each spouse's wages would be his or her separate property.

On Wendy's birthday in 2002, Herb gave Wendy a drawing by a famous artist. Herb paid for the drawing with $15,000 that his parents had given him. Wendy hung the drawing in their bedroom.

In 2003, Wendy opened CoinCo, capitalizing the business with a $10,000 inheritance from her grandfather. Wendy worked at the shop alone every day. Customers appreciated her enthusiasm and ability to obtain special coins at reasonable prices. Over time, Wendy learned she had acquired a number of highly valuable coins. There was also a renewed interest in coin collecting due to the discovery of several boxes of old coins found buried in the area.

Although Wendy's services were worth $40,000 per year, she took an annual salary of $25,000. She also paid $5,000 in household expenses from business earnings each year.

In 2008, Herb and Wendy separated, and Wendy filed for dissolution. At that time, CoinCo was worth $150,000 and the drawing was worth $30,000.

In 2009, before trial, Wendy was disabled by a serious illness and had to be hospitalized. She closed CoinCo while hospitalized, and the value of the business fell to $100,000 by the time of trial. Her hospital bill was not covered by health insurance.

In the dissolution proceeding, Wendy claims the prenuptial agreement is valid and Herb claims it is not.
